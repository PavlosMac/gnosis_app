# Four of Swords

Four blades laid down, edges turned to the wall. This is the truce the mind calls when it has argued itself hoarse — Jupiter, the great benefic, arriving in Libra's final degrees to weigh the season's harvest and find it, at last, balanced. Autumn air, cool and analytic. Chesed in the world of formation: mercy building a chamber where thought can rest without being attacked.

The number four is the room with walls. Here the restless element of Air, all speed and acuity, consents to be housed — and what happens in that housing is study. The scholar's carrel, the laboratory, the cloister, the diagnostician bent over the slide. This card loves the fine grain of things: etiquette, finesse, meticulous attention, the patient watching that sees beneath a surface because it has stopped disturbing that surface. Retreat is not defeat. Withdrawal here is preparation; silence is the instrument of insight. Verdicts come out favourable for those who did their reading.

But a room with walls can become a room without doors. The truce hardens into stalemate; rest calcifies into stagnation. Then the chamber is merely provincial — conservatism dressed as discernment, composure worn as a mask over secrets, pacts made in corridors, delays that no longer serve anything but the comfort of the status quo. Jupiter's expansiveness curdles into never making waves.

Reversed, the walls come down badly. All the finesse is lost: haste replaces patience, indiscretion replaces reserve, meddling replaces solitude. The mind cannot detach enough to see clearly, so it criticizes without purpose and diagnoses wrongly. Details slip. Nothing is prepared.

The card's counsel is simple and difficult: lie down among your swords. Sharpen nothing. Learn. Then rise when the learning, not the fear, says rise.
