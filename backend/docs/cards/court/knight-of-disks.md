# Knight of Disks

He is fire buried in soil — the slow burn beneath the field, the smith's coal, the will that has learned to serve the senses rather than outrun them. Chokmah in Assiah: the first spark of creation come all the way down into mud and money, into the grain of wood and the ledger's neat column. Throned on the Nine of Disks, he sits amid gardens he planted years ago and now, unhurried, harvests.

Look for him in men past their middle years who have made something with their hands: builders, farmers, financiers, physicians, dealers in old and beautiful things. He is credible because he is proven. He is caring in the way stone walls are caring — unglamorous, weatherproof, still standing. Tenacity is his sensuality; he takes pleasure in mending, improving, perfecting, in the patient accumulation of competence.

Yet earth can harden past use. Fire that only serves matter becomes greed, arrogance, a fanaticism of the practical — a man who knows the price of all things and the finesse of none, whose thoroughness curdles into toxicity.

Reversed, the horse moves before the rider is ready: haste, inexperience mistaken for mastery, impractical strategies, dwindling stores, allies drifting away. Sometimes the message is gentler and graver — the body is failing, the harvest is enough. Set down the plough. Rest.
