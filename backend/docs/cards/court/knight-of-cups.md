# Knight of Cups

Fire moving through water — the flame that does not boil the sea but sets it shimmering. This is Chokmah in the world of creation, will bent toward feeling, a man past his thirty-sixth year who has learned that passion is best carried quietly. He is throned upon contentment, and so he can afford grace: the poet, the healer, the one whose intuition arrives before evidence does. He brings good news the way spring brings rain — romance approaching, a marriage, a birth, a friendship that holds. Solitude has refined him rather than soured him.

But water takes the shape of whatever holds it. Without banks, this Knight dissolves: boundaries thin, ideals inflate past any horizon, generosity becomes self-erasure. At his worst he charms in order to deceive, or drowns himself in the cup he meant to offer — addiction, obsession, the slow theatre of self-destruction.

Reversed, the flame goes out entirely. What remains is cold water: a man who has hardened against his own tenderness, hoarding old wounds, refusing to heal, mistaking cruelty for strength and calling his emptiness realism.
